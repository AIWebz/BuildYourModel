Water is made of two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom, so its chemical formula is H2O. Photosynthesis is the process plants use to turn sunlight, water and carbon dioxide into glucose and oxygen. Gravity is the force that pulls objects with mass toward each other. On Earth it accelerates falling objects at about 9.8 meters per second squared. An atom is the smallest unit of a chemical element. It has a nucleus of protons and neutrons surrounded by electrons.
DNA is the molecule that carries the genetic instructions for growth, function and reproduction of all known living things. A cell is the basic unit of life. All living things are made of one or more cells. Light travels at about 299,792 kilometers per second in a vacuum. The Sun is a star made mostly of hydrogen and helium. It produces energy by nuclear fusion.
There are eight planets: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune. Jupiter is the largest planet in the solar system. The Moon is Earth's only natural satellite. It is about 384,400 kilometers away. Water boils at 100 degrees Celsius, or 212 degrees Fahrenheit, at sea level.
Water freezes at 0 degrees Celsius, or 32 degrees Fahrenheit. The common states of matter are solid, liquid, gas and plasma. Energy is the ability to do work. It comes in forms such as kinetic, potential, thermal, chemical and electrical energy, and it cannot be created or destroyed, only changed. Evolution is the change in living things over generations. Natural selection favors traits that help organisms survive and reproduce.
Oxygen is a gas that makes up about 21 percent of air. Our cells use it to release energy from food. Electricity is the flow of electric charge, usually electrons moving through a conductor such as a copper wire. A black hole is a region of space where gravity is so strong that nothing, not even light, can escape. Many form when huge stars collapse. A galaxy is a huge system of stars, gas and dust held together by gravity. We live in the Milky Way, which has hundreds of billions of stars.
The universe is about 13.8 billion years old, dating back to the Big Bang. Earth is about 4.54 billion years old. A light year is the distance light travels in one year, about 9.46 trillion kilometers. The Sun is the closest star to Earth. The next closest is Proxima Centauri, about 4.24 light years away.
Mercury is the smallest planet in the solar system and the closest to the Sun. Venus is the hottest planet. Its thick carbon dioxide atmosphere traps heat, making its surface about 465 degrees Celsius. Mars is called the Red Planet because iron oxide, or rust, in its soil gives it a reddish color. Saturn has the most famous rings, made of ice and rock. Jupiter, Uranus and Neptune have fainter rings too.
A solar eclipse happens when the Moon passes between the Sun and Earth. A lunar eclipse happens when Earth's shadow falls on the Moon. Tides are caused mainly by the Moon's gravity pulling on Earth's oceans, with a smaller effect from the Sun. A volcano is an opening in Earth's crust where molten rock, called magma, gas and ash escape. Pressure from rising magma causes eruptions. Earthquakes happen when tectonic plates in Earth's crust suddenly slip past each other, releasing energy as shaking.
The periodic table organizes all 118 known chemical elements by atomic number and shared properties. Air is about 78 percent nitrogen, 21 percent oxygen, and small amounts of argon, carbon dioxide and other gases. Carbon dioxide, CO2, is a gas made of one carbon and two oxygen atoms. Animals breathe it out, plants use it, and it traps heat in the atmosphere. A molecule is two or more atoms bonded together, such as water, H2O.
An element is a pure substance made of one kind of atom, like gold. A compound is made of two or more elements bonded together, like salt. Friction is a force that resists motion when two surfaces rub against each other. It slows things down and creates heat. Newton's laws: 1, an object stays at rest or in motion unless a force acts on it. 2, force equals mass times acceleration. 3, every action has an equal and opposite reaction. Sound is a vibration that travels as a wave through air, water or solids. In air it travels about 343 meters per second.
A magnet produces a magnetic field that attracts iron and other magnetic metals. Every magnet has a north and a south pole, and opposite poles attract. Bacteria are tiny single celled living organisms, some helpful and some harmful. Viruses are even smaller and can only reproduce inside the cells of a host. In the water cycle, water evaporates, condenses into clouds, falls as precipitation such as rain or snow, and collects in oceans, rivers and the ground. The ozone layer is a part of the upper atmosphere that absorbs most of the Sun's harmful ultraviolet radiation.
Climate change is the long term warming of Earth and shifts in weather patterns, driven mainly by greenhouse gases from burning fossil fuels. The greenhouse effect is when gases like carbon dioxide and methane trap heat in the atmosphere, keeping Earth warm. More of these gases cause more warming. An ecosystem is a community of living things interacting with each other and their environment, such as a forest, a reef or a pond. A food chain shows how energy passes between living things: plants make food from sunlight, herbivores eat plants, and carnivores eat other animals.
A mammal is a warm blooded animal with hair or fur that feeds its young with milk. Humans, dogs, whales and bats are mammals. A reptile is a cold blooded animal with scales that usually lays eggs, such as snakes, lizards, turtles and crocodiles. An insect has six legs and three body parts: head, thorax and abdomen. Ants, bees and butterflies are insects. A spider has eight legs. It is an arachnid, not an insect.
Diamond is the hardest natural substance. It is made of carbon atoms in a strong crystal structure. The pH scale runs from 0 to 14. Below 7 is acidic, like lemon juice, 7 is neutral, like pure water, and above 7 is basic, like soap. A chemical reaction is a process where substances change into new substances, such as burning wood, rusting iron or baking a cake. Mass is how much matter an object has and stays the same everywhere. Weight is the force of gravity on that mass, so it changes on different planets.
Temperature measures how hot or cold something is. Absolute zero, minus 273.15 degrees Celsius, is the coldest possible temperature. Nuclear energy is released by splitting heavy atoms, called fission, or joining light atoms, called fusion. Power plants use fission to boil water and spin turbines. Renewable energy comes from sources that refill naturally, such as sunlight, wind, flowing water and geothermal heat. Mitochondria are the powerhouses of the cell. They turn food and oxygen into energy the cell can use.
Chromosomes are packages of DNA. Humans have 46 chromosomes in 23 pairs, and genes are sections of DNA on them. Metamorphosis is a big change in body form during growth, like a caterpillar becoming a butterfly or a tadpole becoming a frog. The sky looks blue because air molecules scatter blue sunlight more than other colors. This is called Rayleigh scattering. The ocean is salty because rivers carry dissolved minerals from rocks into the sea, and evaporation leaves the salt behind.
A rainbow forms when sunlight is bent and split by raindrops. Its colors are red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo and violet. Hydrogen is the most common element in the universe. It is the lightest element and fuels stars. Some chemical symbols: gold is Au, silver is Ag, iron is Fe, sodium is Na, oxygen is O, carbon is C and hydrogen is H. Earth takes about 365.25 days to orbit the Sun. The extra quarter day is why we add a leap day every four years.
Day and night happen because Earth spins on its axis once about every 24 hours, so each side faces the Sun and then turns away.
